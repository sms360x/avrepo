# Safety Taxonomies: ADREP & HFACS (Nepalese Airspace Context)

This reference documents the structural integration between the International Civil Aviation Organization (ICAO) **ADREP (Accident/Incident Data Reporting)** taxonomy and the **HFACS (Human Factors Analysis and Classification System)** framework, mapped directly against the unique topographic, meteorological, and operational constraints of **Nepalese airspace**.

---

## 1. Nepalese Airspace & Meteorological Constraints

Aviation operations in Nepal take place in one of the world's most unforgiving flight environments. Operational safety taxonomies must account for distinct physical hazards:

| Hazard Dimension | Operational Manifestation in Nepalese Sectors | Critical Airspace / Sectors Affected |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **High Relief Topography** | Deep, narrow valleys with high ridgelines that eliminate standard radar line-of-sight and restrict turning radii. | Solukhumbu (Lukla corridor), Kali Gandaki Gorge (Jomsom route), Karnali basin |
| **Orographic Cloud & VFR-into-IMC** | Rapid afternoon cloud buildup where moisture is forced up mountain slopes, obscuring passes within minutes. | Lamjura Pass, Ghorepani Pass, Simikot ridge passes |
| **Monsoon Convection** | Active squall lines, severe precipitation, low-level windshear, and continuous cloud cover between June and September. | Across Terai plains and mid-hill transit corridors |
| **Valley Fog Inversion** | Dense radiation fog trapping moisture beneath temperature inversion layers, reducing RVR to <100m. | Kathmandu Valley (VNKT) morning operations (Nov–Feb) |
| **Mountain Lee-Waves & Downdrafts** | Severe localized downdrafts and mechanical turbulence exceeding aircraft climb performance margins. | Annapurna and Dhaulagiri gaps, Mustang valley (post-11:00 AM thermal winds) |
| **High Density Altitude** | Reduced air density elevates True Airspeed (TAS), increases ground roll, and degrades single-engine climb gradients. | High-altitude STOL fields: Lukla (9,334 ft), Jomsom (8,976 ft), Simikot (9,246 ft), Syangboche (12,400 ft) |

### High-Priority ADREP Categories in Nepal

| ADREP Code | Occurrence Category | Operational Definition | Direct Manifestation in Nepalese Operations |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **CFIT** | Controlled Flight Into Terrain | Inflight collision with terrain or obstacles under active crew control | Continuing VFR into descending cloud base while crossing high mountain passes (e.g. Lamjura); spatial disorientation due to lack of horizon cues against uniform mountain ridgelines. |
| **LOC-I** | Loss of Control - Inflight | Aircraft deviation from intended flight path beyond aerodynamic control | Severe rotor/lee-wave turbulence encounter in narrow gorges; wing icing during monsoon en-route climbs; low-speed aerodynamic stalls during tight valley reversal turns. |
| **RE** | Runway Excursion | Veer-off or overrun off the runway surface during take-off or landing | Touching down long on short STOL strips (e.g. Lukla 527m runway); hydroplaning on monsoon-contaminated tarmac; loss of directional control in gusty crosswinds at Jomsom. |
| **ARC** | Abnormal Runway Contact | Hard landings, tail strikes, or gear collapse during touchdown | High sink rates caused by sudden sink-cycles over runway thresholds; aggressive flares on steep runway gradients (e.g. Lukla 11.7% upslope). |
| **MAC** | Midair Collision | Collision between aircraft in flight within controlled or uncontrolled airspace | High-density visual traffic in uncontrolled mountain corridors where fixed-wing STOL aircraft and rescue/tourism helicopters share narrow valley transit tracks. |
| **WSTRW** | Windshear / Thunderstorm | Flight encounters with microbursts, extreme convection, or valley wind shifts | Severe thermal katabatic/anabatic wind reversals encountered at high-elevation river valley airstrips. |

## 3. The HFACS Framework in the Nepalese Aviation Context

HFACS analyzes the systemic chain of events across four tiers, adapted specifically to the operational reality of commercial and STOL operations in Nepal:

```
┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│  Level 4: Organizational Influences                        │
│  (Commercial Pressures, STOL Fleet Sourcing, Safety Culture)│
└──────────────────────────────┬──────────────────────────────┘
                               ▼
┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│  Level 3: Unsafe Supervision                                │
│  (Monsoon Dispatching, Junior Crew Pairing, Pass SOP Oversight)│
└──────────────────────────────┬──────────────────────────────┘
                               ▼
┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│  Level 2: Preconditions for Unsafe Acts                     │
│  (Valley Weather Uncertainty, Fatigue, High Altitude Hypoxia)│
└──────────────────────────────┬──────────────────────────────┘
                               ▼
┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│  Level 1: Unsafe Acts of Operators                          │
│  (Pushing VFR into IMC, Late Go-Around Rejection)           │
└─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

### Level 1: Unsafe Acts of Operators
- **Decision Errors**:
  - Pushing forward across an obscured mountain pass instead of executing an early 180° turn back to departure.
  - Committing to land past the stabilized approach decision point on an unpaved or short mountain strip.
- **Skill-Based Errors**:
  - Over-controlling rudder during gusty mountain crosswind flare; improper power/propeller pitch coordination during high-elevation single-engine drift-down.
- **Perceptual Errors**:
  - Somatogravic illusions during high-rate climbs into cloud layers; false visual horizon perceptions against steep Himalayan valley walls.
- **Violations**:
  - Habitual descent below published Sector Safe Altitudes (SSA) or Minimum Off-Route Altitudes (MORA) to maintain visual contact with terrain beneath cloud decks.

### Level 2: Preconditions for Unsafe Acts
- **Environmental Factors**: Rapidly shifting weather fronts; absence of real-time en-route AWOS/weather cameras in remote passes; optical illusions over snow-covered peaks.
- **Condition of Operators**: Acute fatigue from multiple high-altitude rotation sectors (e.g. 5+ Kathmandu–Lukla shuttles in a morning window); mild hypoxia in non-pressurized STOL and helicopter cockpits operating above 10,000 ft.
- **Personnel Factors**: Cockpit authority gradient (steep hierarchical divide inhibiting first officers from challenging senior captains during marginal weather penetrations).

### Level 3: Unsafe Supervision
- **Inadequate Supervision**: Authorizing junior pilots to operate high-risk mountain sectors without adequate route checks.
- **Planned Inappropriate Operations**: Dispatching flights into sectors with known deteriorating weather forecasts to avoid aircraft overnighting out-of-station.
- **Failed to Correct Known Problems**: Neglecting repeat pilot reports of terrain-warning nuisance alerts that lead crews to inhibit EGPWS/TAWS alerts.

### Level 4: Organizational Influences
- **Resource Management**: Operating aging airframes with deferred avionics upgrades; lack of modern high-fidelity simulator training for mountain-specific escape maneuvers.
- **Organizational Climate**: Corporate tolerance for marginal VFR operations to maintain schedule reliability; pressure on pilots from passenger booking backlogs.
- **Operational Processes**: Inadequate Standard Operating Procedures (SOPs) for non-standard visual approach gates in mountainous terrain.

## 4. ADREP-to-HFACS Matrix: Nepalese Incident Mapping

| ADREP Category | Typical Level 1 Unsafe Act | Level 2 Precondition | Level 3 Supervisory Gap | Level 4 Organizational Factor |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **CFIT** *(Mountain Pass)* | Decision: Pushing VFR below valley ceiling into IMC | Spatial disorientation; lack of pass weather cameras | Dispatching during questionable afternoon buildup | Commercial pressure to complete turnaround flights |
| **LOC-I** *(Gorge Turbulence)* | Skill: Inappropriate aerodynamic stall recovery | Severe thermal windshear; high density altitude | Inadequate upset recovery training (UPRT) in mountain conditions | Simulator curriculum omitting mountain turbulence modeling |
| **RE / ARC** *(STOL Landing)* | Decision: Continuing unstabilized approach past commit point | High tailwind component; wet, contaminated runway | Failure to enforce company stabilized approach criteria | Policy penalizing diversion fuel expenditure |
| **MAC** *(VFR Valley Corridor)* | Perceptual: Failure to spot conflicting traffic visually | High workload in narrow valley; non-ADS-B traffic | Lack of formalized one-way routing separation protocols | Infrastructure gap in valley radar and ADS-B ground stations |

## 5. Analytical Tagging Standard for Nepalese Operators

When logging safety occurrences under CAR-19 SMS reporting systems, use standardized compound taxonomy tags:

- **Format**: `[ADREP-CODE] / [HFACS-TIER] / [NEPAL-HAZARD-SPECIFIER]`
- **Examples**:
  - `CFIT / L1-DEC-01 (VFR into IMC) / TERRAIN-PASS-OBSCURATION (Lamjura)`
  - `RE / L1-SKL-02 (Long Touchdown) / STOL-SLOPED-RUNWAY (VNLK-RWY06)`
  - `LOC-I / L2-ENV-TURB / MOUNTAIN-LEE-WAVE (Mustang-Kagbeni)`
