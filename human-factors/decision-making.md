# Aeronautical Decision Making (ADM) in Nepalese Mountain Operations

Aeronautical Decision Making (ADM) is a systematic approach to the mental processes used by flight crews, dispatchers, and air traffic controllers to consistently determine the safest course of action. In Nepal's high-elevation and micro-climatic operating environment, standard decision models must be applied with rigorous terrain-aware discipline.

---

## 1. Dynamic Decision Frameworks Mapped to Nepal Sectors

### 1.1 The DECIDE Model in Mountain En-Route Scenarios

```
    Detect ───► Estimate ───► Choose ───► Identify ───► Do ───► Evaluate
       ▲                                                           │
       └───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

The DECIDE framework applied to an en-route visual flight encounter in a typical Nepalese mountain valley:

1. **Detect (D)**: The crew detects lowering cloud base and wisps obscuring the high ridgelines approaching **Lamjura Pass** (11,500 ft) on the Kathmandu–Lukla corridor.
2. **Estimate (E)**: The crew estimates that terrain clearance margins will be compressed to <500 ft AGL within 3 minutes; continuing forward under VFR violates CAAN's 1,500 ft ceiling directive and risks CFIT.
3. **Choose (C)**: The crew chooses an uncompromising safety objective: **immediate 180° escape reversal turn** before passing the geographic commit point in the valley.
4. **Identify (I)**: Identify the widest valley section to execute a maximum-performance coordinated 180° turn, configure flap setting for lower stall speed ($V_{S}$), and notify flight operations control / ATC.
5. **Do (D)**: Smoothly execute the reversal turn, wings level, maintaining positive visual contact with valley floor.
6. **Evaluate (E)**: Verify terrain clearance is restored, clear airspace is ahead toward Phaplu / Kathmandu, and assess diversion fuel reserves.

### 1.2 The FOR-DEC Model in Multi-Crew Airline Operations

Widely mandated in Nepalese multi-crew cockpits (ATR 72, Dash 8 Q400, Airbus A320/A330):

| Stage | Action Step | Operational Application in Nepal (e.g. VNKT Winter Morning Fog) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **F** | **Facts** | Runway Visual Range (RVR) at Tribhuvan Int'l (VNKT) drops to 300m due to valley radiation fog. Onboard fuel allows 45 minutes holding before reaching divert fuel for Bhairahawa (VNPW) / Kolkata. |
| **O** | **Options** | Option 1: Enter holding pattern over SIMARA (SM) waiting for fog burn-off.<br>Option 2: Divert immediately to Gautam Buddha Int'l (VNPW) or Pokhara (VNPK). |
| **R** | **Risks & Benefits** | Holding risks burning into final diversion reserve if valley fog does not lift; diverting early preserves fuel reserves and provides passenger certainty. |
| **D** | **Decision** | Pilot-in-Command (PIC) briefs First Officer: "We hold for maximum 20 minutes over SIMARA. If RVR remains below 800m at time +20, we execute immediate diversion to VNPW." |
| **E** | **Execution** | Advise Kathmandu Control of holding time limitation; input diversion route to VNPW into FMS; brief Cat I instrument approach into VNPW. |
| **C** | **Check / Countermeasures**| Monitor fuel totalizer burn rate every 5 minutes; verify meteorological trends at VNPW; enforce diversion trigger upon reaching commit fuel. |

## 2. Pre-Flight Operational Risk Tools

### 2.1 The PAVE Checklist: Nepalese Operational Grounding

| Pillar | Nepal Operational Assessment Focus |
| :--- | :--- |
| **P — Pilot-in-Command** | - Has the crew flown this specific mountain sector within the last 30 days?<br>- Valid STOL route qualification endorsement for critical airfields (Lukla VNLK, Jomsom VNJS, Simikot VNST).<br>- Fatigue status after multiple morning shuttle rotations.<br>- Steep gradient hierarchy: Is the First Officer empowered to challenge the Captain? |
| **A — Aircraft** | - True Airspeed (TAS) vs. Groundspeed compensation for high density altitude.<br>- Single-engine service ceiling vs. Minimum Off-Route Altitude (MORA).<br>- De-icing equipment functionality for winter/monsoon cloud penetrations.<br>- EGPWS / TAWS Class A operational status (no deferred MEL items permitted on mountain routes). |
| **V — enVironment** | - Morning thermal wind shifts: Jomsom 11:00 AM tailwind cutoff; Lukla afternoon cloud buildup.<br>- Monsoon precipitation accumulation: Runway friction coefficients on sloped STOL runways.<br>- Availability of reliable weather observations from remote pass webcams and local ground agents. |
| **E — External Pressures** | - Backlog of stranded high-paying tourists at mountain airfields due to previous day weather delays.<br>- VIP or government charter expectations requesting landing in marginal weather.<br>- Airline commercial dispatch pressure to return aircraft to base before night curfew. |

### 2.2 The IMSAFE Self-Assessment (Mountain Sector Adaptation)

```
┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│  I  Illness       - Sinus or eustachian tube congestion     │
│  M  Medication    - Altitude sickness meds (Acetazolamide)  │
│  S  Stress        - Mountain sector pressure & turnaround   │
│  A  Alcohol       - 24-hr zero tolerance (BAC 0.000%)       │
│  F  Fatigue       - Circadian low & high-altitude hypoxia   │
│  E  Emotion/Eating- Dehydration at high elevation (dry air) │
└─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

- **Illness (Sinus Health)**: Rapid pressure changes during STOL descents (e.g. descending from 12,000 ft cruise to Lukla at 9,334 ft in minutes) risk severe barotrauma, ear block, and incapacitating vertigo.
- **Medication**: Use of unauthorized sedatives, cold medications, or unapproved altitude sickness prophylactic pills without AME clearance is strictly prohibited.
- **Fatigue & Hypoxia**: Flight crews operating unpressurized Twin Otters or helicopters above 10,000 ft experience accelerated cognitive degradation, tunnel vision, and degraded reaction times. Supplemental oxygen must be utilized according to FOR mandates.

## 3. Hazardous Attitudes & Cockpit Antidotes in Nepal Operations

| Hazardous Attitude | Typical Cockpit Mindset in Mountain Flights | CAAN / Operator Antidote |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Anti-Authority** | *"CAAN's 5 km en-route visibility rule is too conservative. We know this valley by memory and don't need published minima."* | **"Follow the rules; they are written from accident investigation lessons."** |
| **Impulsivity** | *"Weather is closing in fast! We need to dive down right now to sneak under this cloud deck before the valley closes."* | **"Not so fast. Think first: climb to safety altitude or turn back immediately."** |
| **Invulnerability** | *"Accidents in the passes happen to inexperienced crews, not to seasoned mountain veterans like us."* | **"It could happen to anyone. High Himalayan terrain does not forgive overconfidence."** |
| **Macho** | *"A real mountain captain doesn't turn back for a little rain or fog. The passengers paid to reach Lukla."* | **"Taking chances is unprofessional. Diverting or canceling is the hallmark of professional airmanship."** |
| **Resignation** | *"We are boxed in by clouds on both sides of the ridge. There's nothing we can do now except pray we clear the pass."* | **"I am not helpless. Level wings, pitch for best climb ($V_{Y}$), apply maximum continuous power, trust instruments, and execute immediate escape profile."** |

## 4. Threat and Error Management (TEM) in Mountain Operations

### 4.1 Threat Inundation & Mitigation

```
┌──────────────────┐       ┌──────────────────┐       ┌────────────────────────┐
│     Threat       │  ──►  │      Error       │  ──►  │ Undesired Aircraft     │
│ (Valley Downburst│       │ (Delayed Power   │       │ State (Low Energy      │
│  / Pass Fog)     │       │  Application)    │       │  on STOL Final)        │
└──────────────────┘       └──────────────────┘       └───────────┬────────────┘
                                                                  ▼
                                                      ┌────────────────────────┐
                                                      │  Immediate Go-Around   │
                                                      │   or Escape Maneuver   │
                                                      └────────────────────────┘
```

1. **Environmental Threat**: Severe windshear or downdrafts over mountain thresholds.
   - *Countermeasure*: Crew briefing identifying commit points; continuous monitoring of energy state ($V_{REF}$ + wind additive).
2. **Operational Threat**: High cockpit authority gradient between senior instructor and first officer.
   - *Countermeasure*: Enforcing CAAN-mandated sterile cockpit rules and standard challenge-and-response callouts (e.g. mandatory go-around call if sink rate exceeds 800 ft/min below 500 ft AGL).
3. **Undesired Aircraft State (UAS)**: Aircraft below glide path and slow on short final into a high-altitude airstrip.
   - *Recovery Action*: Immediate go-around before passing the geographic commit point; if beyond commit point on one-way airstrips (e.g. Lukla RWY 06), apply go-around power instantly while terrain escape maneuvers remain viable.
