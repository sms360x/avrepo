# Aeronautical Decision Making (ADM) & Operational Frameworks

Aeronautical Decision Making (ADM) is a systematic approach to the mental process used by pilots, dispatchers, and air traffic controllers to consistently determine the best course of action in response to a given set of operational circumstances.

---

## 1. Structured Decision Frameworks

### 1.1 The DECIDE Model

The DECIDE model provides a six-step iterative framework designed for dynamic inflight problem solving:

```
    Detect ───► Estimate ───► Choose ───► Identify ───► Do ───► Evaluate
       ▲                                                           │
       └───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

1. **Detect (D)**: The pilot detects that a change has occurred (e.g. unexpected cloud buildup in valley pass, hydraulic pressure gauge drop).
2. **Estimate (E)**: Estimate the significance of the change on flight safety and mission continuation.
3. **Choose (C)**: Choose an outcome objective (e.g. divert to alternate, initiate immediate climb to MORA).
4. **Identify (I)**: Identify actions that could successfully control the change.
5. **Do (D)**: Execute the chosen operational action.
6. **Evaluate (E)**: Evaluate the effect of the action to ensure the threat is eliminated or mitigated.

---

### 1.2 The FOR-DEC Model

Widely adopted in commercial multi-crew airline cockpits:

| Step | Action Item | Flight Deck Operational Application |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **F** | **Facts** | Gather raw telemetry, weather reports, fuel onboard, system status messages (EICAS/ECAM). |
| **O** | **Options** | Brainstorm realistic alternatives (hold, divert, request priority, continue with degraded systems). |
| **R** | **Risks & Benefits** | Weigh risk factors (runway length, weather at alternate, approach aids) against operational benefits. |
| **D** | **Decision** | Pilot-in-Command (PIC) reaches clear, communicated determination after first officer input. |
| **E** | **Execution** | Program flight management system (FMS), brief approach, request ATC clearance, configure aircraft. |
| **C** | **Check / Countermeasures**| Continuously verify system behavior, monitor fuel burn down to final reserve, re-evaluate if conditions shift. |

## 2. Pre-Flight Risk Assessment Tools

### 2.1 The PAVE Checklist

Divides operational risk factors into four fundamental pillars:

- **P — Pilot-in-Command**: Physical fitness, currency, recency of experience on specific airframe type, fatigue state.
- **A — Aircraft**: Airworthiness, MEL/CDL deferred defect items, fuel reserves, climb performance margins under density altitude.
- **V — enVironment**: Weather ceilings, icing levels, terrain elevations (Himalayan passes), runway contamination, airport infrastructure.
- **E — External Pressures**: VIP passengers, commercial schedule urgency, "get-there-itis", family commitments.

### 2.2 The IMSAFE Personal Assessment

Every crew member must evaluate personal operational fitness prior to reporting for duty:

| Letter | Evaluation Dimension | Self-Query Standard |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **I** | **Illness** | Do I have any symptoms, even mild acute upper respiratory illness affecting sinus equalization? |
| **M** | **Medication** | Am I taking prescription or over-the-counter drugs unapproved by the aviation medical examiner? |
| **S** | **Stress** | Am I experiencing psychological pressure, personal financial worries, or emotional strain? |
| **A** | **Alcohol** | Have I consumed alcohol within 24 hours? Is BAC strictly 0.000%? |
| **F** | **Fatigue** | Am I adequately rested, considering sleep debt and circadian low transitions? |
| **E** | **Eating / Emotion** | Have I maintained proper nutrition, hydration, and emotional stability for the sortie? |

## 3. Hazardous Attitudes and Operational Antidotes

FAA and ICAO studies identify five characteristic cognitive biases that compromise flight safety:

| Hazardous Attitude | Typical Flight Deck Mindset | Recognized Antidote |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Anti-Authority** | *"Don't tell me what to do; the rules are overly bureaucratic."* | **"Follow the rules; they are usually right."** |
| **Impulsivity** | *"Do something quickly! We can't just sit here."* | **"Not so fast; think first, verify action."** |
| **Invulnerability** | *"Accidents happen to other operators, not to me."* | **"It could happen to me; respect the envelope."** |
| **Macho** | *"I can prove I can land in these conditions."* | **"Taking chances is foolish and unprofessional."** |
| **Resignation** | *"What's the use? There's nothing I can do now."* | **"I am not helpless; I can make a difference."** |

## 4. Threat and Error Management (TEM) Integration

TEM operates across three progressive defensive rings:

1. **Threat Management**: Anticipate environmental threats (e.g., gusty winds at Jomsom / VNJS, valley downdrafts) and organizational threats (tight turnarounds) through briefings.
2. **Error Management**: Detect execution slips, lapses, or procedural deviations before they result in an undesired aircraft state.
3. **Undesired Aircraft State (UAS) Management**: Rapid recovery when aircraft conditions (e.g. altitude deviation, high approach speed) deviate from stabilized criteria. Execute immediate go-around without hesitation.
